# Animation catalog

The full behavior graph is 54 authored animations (`src/animations_data.c`,
generated by `tools/gen_animations.py` from `tools/esheep_animations.xml`),
connected by 96 weighted, context-filtered transitions. This is a grouped,
human-readable reference; for the raw data see:

- `./esheep --list-animations` — the 54 IDs and names, straight from the
  active behavior graph (built-in or a `--package` override).
- `./esheep --list-transitions` — all 96 transitions: index, stable ID,
  source animation, kind (`sequence`/`border`/`gravity`/`child`), context,
  probability, and target. This is what `tools/review_transitions.py` reads
  to drive playback; see "Reviewing a scene" below.
- `python3 tools/render_animation_catalog.py --output FILE.png` — a visual
  contact sheet of every animation's frames, with frame IDs and child
  mappings labeled.

## Groups

**Locomotion**
`walk`(1), `run`(7), `run_begin`(35), `run_end`(36) — ground movement.
`rotate1a`/`rotate1b`(2, 3) — the coin-flip turnaround on a direction change.
`jump`(25), `jump_down`/`jump_down2`/`jump_down3`(44, 45, 46) — hopping off a
ledge.

**Falling**
`fall`(5), `fall fast`(6), `fall soft`(9), `fall hard`(10) — different fall
speeds and landings on open ground.
`fall_wina`–`fall_wind`(51–54) — falling off or around a window edge
specifically.

**Climbing / window and top surfaces**
`vertical_walk_up`/`vertical_walk_down`/`vertical_walk_over`(37, 41, 42) —
climbing a vertical edge (screen side or a detected window's side).
`top_walk`/`top_walk2`/`top_walk3`(38–40) — walking along the top of a
window or panel.
`look_down`(43) — peering over a ledge.
`walk_win2`/`walk_task2`(49, 50) — walking while docked on a window or the
taskbar.

**Sleep**
`sleep1a`/`sleep1b`(15, 16), `sleep2a`/`sleep2b`(17, 18),
`sleep3a`/`sleep3b`(19, 20) — three distinct dozing-off sequences.

**Reactions and gags**
`boing`(8) — the bounce/squash-stretch reaction.
`sync`(14) — a small idle sync beat.
`pissa`/`pissb`(11, 12) — a two-part gag.
`kill`(13) — a dizzy/defeated reaction.
`batha`, `bathb`, `bathw`, `bathz`, `bathc`, `bathd`(21–24, 47, 48) — the
bath sequence.
`eat`(26) — grazing.
`blacksheepa`–`blacksheepz`(28–34: a, b, c, v, w, y, z) — the black-sheep
transformation gag.

**Interaction**
`drag`(4) — being picked up by the mouse.

## Multi-sprite (child) scenes

Most animations render one sprite. Three authored `<child>` records
additionally spawn a second, independently-animated sprite alongside the
parent — these are the `kind == "child"` rows in `--list-transitions`
(currently transition indices 94-96):

| Parent | Child | What it is | Positioning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `eat`(26) | `flower`(27) | A flower grows where the sheep is grazing | Relative to the parent (`imageX`/`imageY` in the expression) |
| `blacksheepa`(28) | `blacksheepv`(31) | The UFO/companion in the black-sheep gag | Relative to the parent, and keeps moving further away each tick per its own authored animation |
| `batha`(21) | `bathw`(23) | The bath prop | **Absolute**, monitor-scale position (`screenW`/`areaH` in the expression) — enters from off the right edge of the screen independent of where the parent is, the same convention `<spawn>` points use |

`src/main.c`'s `update_child_animation()` distinguishes these two placement
styles by whether the expression references a monitor-scale variable
(`screenW`/`screenH`/`areaW`/`areaH`); everything else is treated as a small
delta from the parent. See the comment above that check, and the commit
that introduced it, for the two rendering bugs this distinction fixes
(`gtk_widget_set_size_request()` fighting the composited window's growth,
and an absolute-style expression being misread as a tiny relative one).

## Reviewing a scene

```sh
tools/review_transitions.py            # play every transition in order
tools/review_transitions.py 17         # play transition 17 only
tools/review_transitions.py 17 3000    # ...for 3000ms instead of the 1800ms default
```

For each transition, the tool reads `--list-transitions`, then launches the
binary with either `--review-animation TARGET` (most transitions — jumps
straight to the destination animation) or, for a `child`-kind transition,
`--review-parent SOURCE` (plays the *parent* animation from the top, so its
authored child spawns naturally instead of being shown out of context).
Pass `--package PATH` (and optionally `--sprite PATH`) to review a custom
behavior package instead of the built-in graph.

`tests/test_child_scene_rendering.py` is the automated version of this for
the three child scenes: it actually renders each one under Xvfb and checks
that both sprites land on screen at a sane size and position, rather than
only validating the authored XML/generated data (which is what
`tests/test_child_animations.py` covers).
